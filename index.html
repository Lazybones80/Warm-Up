Guitar Warm-Up Lab

A focused warm-up library for getting both hands awake without turning the first ten minutes into mindless speed exercises. Start with the spider, then choose the exercises that match what you want to improve today.

Quick start: 12 minutes

  • 2 min: relaxed chromatic movement
  • 4 min: permutation spider
  • 2 min: legato ladder
  • 2 min: string-crossing pattern
  • 2 min: musical application in C major
Rule: clean, loose, even, and musical beats a higher metronome number.
Rules Spider Independence Legato Crossing Shifting Chords

How to use the exercises

Warm up the movement first, then add pressure, speed, accents, and position changes one at a time. Every exercise below tells you exactly what the left hand, picking hand, and metronome should do.

Read before playing

1. Set the body up

  1. Put the thumb roughly behind the neck, opposite the first or second finger.
  2. Let the wrist stay long. Do not collapse it toward the palm.
  3. Use only enough fingertip pressure to make the note speak.
  4. Keep unused fingers close to the strings instead of lifting them high.

2. Set the metronome

Start at a tempo where every note is boringly clean. For sixteenth notes, 60 BPM gives you four notes per click.

Advance: add only 5 BPM after two clean repetitions with no tightening in the shoulders, forearm, or thumb.

2. Finger independence permutations

Once the first pattern feels familiar, rotate the order. The point is to make every finger combination feel equally ordinary.

The real challenge

Use these six permutations

1–2–3–4Basic ladder
1–3–2–4Middle-finger split
1–2–4–3Pinky before ring
1–3–4–2Ring and pinky together
1–4–2–3Wide index-to-pinky jump
1–4–3–2Descending inner order

How to practise them

  1. Pick one pattern for one minute.
  2. Play up six strings and back down.
  3. Repeat at the same tempo with the next pattern.
  4. On the final pass, accent the first note of every four-note group.
Why accents help: they reveal whether the other three fingers are arriving evenly or whether the first finger is doing all the work.

3. Legato ladder

This wakes up the fretting hand without relying on the pick for every note. It teaches you to create sound with a small hammer-on and a controlled pull-off.

Left-hand strength

One-string ladder

e|5h6p5--5h7p5--5h8p5--|
B|----------------------|
G|----------------------|
D|----------------------|
A|----------------------|
E|----------------------|

h = hammer-on. p = pull-off. Pick only the first 5 of each group.

What the fingers should do

  • Hammer with the fingertip landing close to the fret.
  • Do not yank the string sideways during the pull-off.
  • Keep the unused fingers relaxed instead of clenching.
  • Move through all six strings, then reverse the direction.
Sound test: the hammered and pulled notes should be almost as loud as the picked note. If they disappear, slow down and use cleaner contact rather than more force.

4. String-crossing engine

Many players can fret the notes but lose synchronization when the pick has to cross strings. This exercise makes the crossing part of the warm-up.

Picking accuracy

Two notes per string

Play down the strings and back up. Use alternate picking continuously so the first note on each new string does not become an accidental downstroke.

Think of the pick as a pendulum. The string change happens inside the same down-up motion; do not stop the hand at the boundary.

Variation: play three notes per string, then four. The odd-number group changes which pick direction begins the next string.

5. Position-shift warm-up

This adds controlled movement up the neck. A guitarist who only practises inside one box can feel clean but become unreliable as soon as a phrase requires a shift.

Neck movement

Three-note diagonal

e|----------------5-7-8-|
B|------------5-7-8-----|
G|--------5-7-8---------|
D|----5-7-8-------------|
A|5-7-8-----------------|
E|5-7-8-----------------|

After the first pass, shift the pattern up two frets: 7–9–10. Keep the hand shape compact and move from the arm, not by twisting the wrist.

Shift without a gap

  1. Play the final note of the old position.
  2. Release pressure while the note finishes ringing.
  3. Move the thumb and fingers together.
  4. Land before the next beat, then play the new note.
Listen for: the shift should sound like one phrase, not a dropped connection between two exercises.

6. Chord-grip and release

Finish with a musical warm-up. This connects the finger work to chord changes and teaches the tension-and-release habit you need for clean rhythm playing.

Musical transfer

C major → G major → A minor → F major

CPlace the shape. Strum once.
GRelease, move, then place.
Am → FKeep the pulse and breathe.

Play four slow downstrokes per chord. Then use one bar per chord with a simple down-up pattern.

Release drill

  1. Form the chord without strumming.
  2. Strum once and listen for every note.
  3. Relax the fretting pressure while keeping fingers hovering over the shape.
  4. Re-form the chord and repeat.
The purpose: you learn to remove unnecessary tension between chord changes, so speed comes from efficient movement rather than force.

Choose the right version today

Use the warm-up as a diagnostic. The exercise that exposes the most unevenness is usually the one worth practising slowly.

Daily choice

If the left hand feels stiff

Do the spider at a slow tempo, then the legato ladder. Concentrate on low finger height and minimum pressure.

If the right hand feels unreliable

Do the two-note string crossing and accent every first note of a string. Keep the picking motion small.

If changes feel clumsy

Do the chord-grip and release drill. Leave the metronome slow enough to hear the moment tension enters your hand.

If everything feels easy

Keep the same pattern but shift it through several positions, add accents, and play it with a backing groove. Difficulty should remain controlled.

Stop signal: sharp pain, tingling, numbness, or pain that lingers means stop and rest. A warm-up should make movement feel freer, not leave your hand irritated.
